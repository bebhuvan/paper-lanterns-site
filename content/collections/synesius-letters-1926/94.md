This obviously intriguing personage was opposed to Synesius on the question most dear to his heart, namely the exemption of foreigners from service in the Roman army, and the enrolling of all Romans. The arguments in favour of this course he had given with great eloquence in his address to the emperor Arcadius (see Introduction). Another reform which Synesius desired was the abolition of the military commandership in the province, and the transferring of his functions to the Count of Egypt—as in the past.
