He met Mrs. McLehose on his second visit to Edinburgh, and a warm affection sprang up between them. But Burns’s “marriage lines” to Jean Armour held him in a relation which the law considered binding, and Mrs. McLehose had a husband in the West Indies, who had cruelly left her to a life of poverty and struggle with her children in Edinburgh. Thus there was no hope of marriage between them; and, after an ardent correspondence, from which the following letters are taken, they separated.
