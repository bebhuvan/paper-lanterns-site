There are few characters in the naval annals of England that excite such personal sympathy and interest as Lord Nelson. The enthusiasm which was felt for him by every sailor under his command communicates itself to all who read his life. He was a man perfectly brave, yet tender to excess; his men said of him that “ he was brave as a lion yet as gentle as a lamb.” His feelings were so acute that when any harsh discipline was enforced on board his ship he suffered from it as much as if he had been a woman. Such a nature as this was likely to be strongly swayed by the passion which seized him in the prime of manhood, at the height of his fame. That his love for Lady Hamilton was most genuine and sincere, no one can doubt. It was so sweeping and absolute that nothing in his life could stand against it. It is not in my power to do much honour to her in this place, but I have invited all the admirals and all the captains who have the happiness of knowing you, and, of course, of experiencing your kindness while in the Mediterranean.
