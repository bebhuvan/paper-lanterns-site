      "t": "Do not expect much of a letter this time, for I am tired out with all the excitement of the past few days. . . . As long as Liszt is here I cannot do much work, and do not know how I am going to be ready by Holy Thursday, for I am with him nearly all day. He said yesterday: ‘I feel as if I had known you twenty years,’ and I have just the same feeling towards him. We have arrived at the stage of being as rude as we like to each other, and I have frequent cause to avail myself of the privilege, as he is really too capricious and has been spoilt by his stay in Vienna. I shall not be able to squeeze into this letter all I have to tell you of our first meeting at Dresden, the concert, my railway journey back yesterday, last night’s concert, and this morning’s rehearsal for the next. I have at last had a chance of hearing Liszt’s wonderful playing, which alternates between a fine frenzy and the utmost delicacy. But his world is not mine, Clärchen. Art, as we know it—you when you play, I when I compose—has an intimate charm that is worth more to me than all Liszt's splendour and tinsel! But enough for to-day. You understand all I would say."
