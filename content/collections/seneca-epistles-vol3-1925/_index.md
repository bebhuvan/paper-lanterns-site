  "Written during Lucius Annaeus Seneca's final retirement from Nero's court in A.D. 64–65 and addressed to his friend Gaius Lucilius Iunior, Volume III concludes the extant corpus of the Epistulae Morales with Letters XCIII–CXXIV. Across these thirty-two letters Seneca reflects on the death of the Neapolitan philosopher Metronax and the fullness rather than length of human life (Epistle XCIII, scan leaf 15, p. 3), develops his monumental twin treatises on ethical precepts and foundational Stoic doctrines (Epistles XCIV and XCV, scan leaves 23 and 71, pp. 11 and 59), appraises the philosophical prose of Papirius Fabianus (Epistle C, scan leaf 161, p. 149), recounts his retreat to his country estate at Nomentum with his wife Paulina alongside the examples of Socrates and Cato the Younger (Epistle CIV, scan leaf 203, p. 191), recalls his youthful asceticism under his teachers Attalus and Sotion (Epistle CVIII, scan leaf 241, p. 229), diagnoses corrupt literary style as a mirror of moral character through the case of Gaius Maecenas (Epistle CXIV, scan leaves 313–315, pp. 301–303), and closes the collection with the demonstration that human Good is perfected solely through reason (Epistle CXXIV, scan leaves 447–461 odd, pp. 435–449).",
