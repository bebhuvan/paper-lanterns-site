Vegetation also, which will develop into grain and fruits, has a special constitution when young and scarcely peeping over the tops of the furrows, another when it is strengthened and stands upon a stalk which is soft but strong enough to bear its weight, and still another when the colour changes to yellow, prophesies threshing-time, and hardens in the ear— no matter what may be the constitution into which the plant comes, it keeps it, and conforms thereto. Thus, although each has at different times a different constitution, the adaptation of each to its constitution is the same. Therefore, the child is adapted to that constitution which is his at the present moment of childhood, not to that which will be his in youth.
