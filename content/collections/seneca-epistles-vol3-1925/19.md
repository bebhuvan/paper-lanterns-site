He, however, who has practised philosophy to effect his own cure, becomes high-souled, full of confidence, invincible, and greater as you draw near him. This phenomenon is seen in the case of high mountains, which appear less lofty when beheld from afar, but which prove clearly how high the peaks are when you come near them ; such, my dear Lucilius, is our true philosopher, true by his acts and not by his tricks. He stands in a high place, worthy of admiration, lofty, and really great. He does not stretch himself or walk on tiptoe like those who seek to improve their height by deceit, wishing to seem taller than they really are ; he is content with his own greatness. And why should he not be content with having grown to such a height that Fortune cannot reach her hands to it ? He is therefore above earthly things, equal to himself under all conditions, —whether the current of life runs free, or whether he is tossed and travels on troubled and desperate seas ; but this steadfastness cannot be gained through such hair-splittings as I have just mentioned.
