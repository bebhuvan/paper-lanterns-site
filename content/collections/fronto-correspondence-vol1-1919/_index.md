  "Rediscovered in 1815 by Cardinal Angelo Mai on a palimpsest divided between the Ambrosian Library at Milan and the Vatican Library at Rome (Preface, scan leaf 13; Introduction, scan leaves 19–24), the correspondence of Marcus Cornelius Fronto (c. A.D. 100–c. 166/170) offers an intimate portrait of Roman literary and court life under the Antonines. Volume I spans A.D. 139–161 (scan leaves 67–373), preserving Fronto's letters to and from his imperial pupil Marcus Aurelius (as Caesar and as Emperor), alongside letters involving Antoninus Pius, Lucius Verus, Domitia Lucilla, Appian, Herodes Atticus, and Roman friends.",
  "Charles Reginald Haines (1856–1935) edited the Latin and Greek text anew and produced this first English translation in 1919 (title leaf, scan leaf 11; Preface, scan leaf 13). Volume I includes Haines's Preface (scan leaves 13–14), Introduction and essay 'Fronto, the Orator and the Man' (scan leaves 17–50), Bibliography (scan leaves 51–59), Chronological Table (scan leaves 60–62), Sigla (scan leaf 63), and 152 translated letters and speeches across 154 English facing-page leaves (scan leaves 67–373). This Paper Lanterns edition preserves Haines's historical apparatus, polytonic Greek quotations, textual footnotes, and authentic epistolary layouts verbatim."
