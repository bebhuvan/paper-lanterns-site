      "t": "MR. LESCKE, I should have been glad to have heard some news from the church, for of the world I have daily too much. Unum necesse est. Though it was not my good hap to see you at my coming away, yet I left my well wishes and shall be glad to hear of you and from you. Much I cannot do, but both your place (to which I ever owed reverence), your sincere carriage (to which I must testify) command me to offer anything in my power, which you shall freely use when you will make trial. I know too well how slightly the ministry is regarded ; but he that considers that saying of our Saviour : he that despiseth me, despiseth Him that sent me, will know God takes the injuries offered to His delegates as done to Him. I write not that I doubt any such measure toward you (your own gravity will withstand it) ; but that I may let you know my desire to carry straw to God's building, and that I had rather wait on the temple than in the courts of Princes."
