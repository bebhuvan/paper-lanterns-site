      "t": "You may readily trust, my dear sir, that any exertion in my power is heartily at your service. But one thing I must hint to you: the very name of Peter Pindar is of great service to your publication, so get a verse from him now and then, though I have no objection as well as I can to bear the burden of the business. You know that my pretensions to musical taste are merely a few of Nature’s instincts, untaught and untutored by art. For this reason many musical compositions, particularly where much of the merit lies in counterpoint, however they may transport and ravish the ears of your connoisseurs, affect my simple lug no otherwise than melodious din. On the other hand, by way of amends, I am delighted with many little melodies, which the learned musician despises as silly and insipid. I do not know whether the old air “ Hey tuttie taitie,” may rank among this number, but well I know that, with Fraser’s hautboy, it has often filled my eye with tears. There is a tradition which I have met with in many places of Scotland, that it was Robert Bruce’s march at the battle of Bannockburn. This thought in my solitary wanderings, warmed me to a pitch of enthusiasm on the theme of liberty and independence, which I threw into a kind of Scottish ode, fitted to the air, that one might suppose to be the gallant Royal Scot’s address to his heroic followers on that eventful morning."
