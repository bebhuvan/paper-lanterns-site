  "Composed after his retirement from Nero's court in A.D. 62 during the years A.D. 63–65 (Introduction, scan leaves 16–18, pp. viii–x), the Moral Epistles of Lucius Annaeus Seneca (4 B.C.–A.D. 65; scan leaves 15–17, pp. vii–ix) are addressed to his younger friend Lucilius, a Campanian eques and imperial procurator in Sicily (scan leaf 17, p. ix). Volume I presents Epistles I–LXV: philosophical letters moving from everyday Roman scenes—saving time (Epistle I, scan leaf 27), discursiveness in reading (Epistle II, scan leaf 31), crowds and gladiatorial shows (Epistle VII, scan leaf 53), the humane treatment of slaves (Epistle XLVII, scan leaf 325), Baiae, Vatia's villa, the din of the Roman baths, and the Naples tunnel (Epistles LI, LV–LVII, scan leaves 361, 389–397)—to Stoic reflections on friendship, old age, illness, and death, enriched with sayings of Epicurus and verses from Vergil and Publilius Syrus.",
