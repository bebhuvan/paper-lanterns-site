  "Published between 20 B.C. and c. 13 B.C. (Introduction, scan leaves 18–19, pp. xii–xiii), the twenty-three verse letters of Quintus Horatius Flaccus (Horace, 65–8 B.C.) transform the Roman poetic epistle into an intimate vehicle for ethical reflection, friendship, rural retirement, and literary criticism. Book I (twenty epistles, 20 B.C.; scan leaves 283–427, pp. 247–391) opens with Horace's farewell to lyric verse addressed to his patron Gaius Maecenas (Epist. I. i, scan leaves 284–295) and ranges across moral letters to young Maximus Lollius on reading Homer at Praeneste (I. ii, scan leaves 296–303), inquiries to Julius Florus on Tiberius's Eastern expedition (I. iii, scan leaves 305–309), console-and-counsel notes to the elegist Albius Tibullus (I. iv, scan leaves 311–313), a model letter of introduction to the future emperor Tiberius (I. ix, scan leaves 345–347), praise of Sabine country peace to Aristius Fuscus and to his own farm bailiff (I. x and I. xiv, scan leaves 349–355, 373–377), and the famous closing address to his own book (I. xx, scan leaves 423–427).",
  "Book II (scan leaves 428–477, pp. 392–441) and the Ars Poetica or Epistle to the Pisos (scan leaves 478–525, pp. 442–489) present Horace's mature literary criticism: his defence of modern Roman poetry addressed to the Emperor Augustus (II. i, scan leaves 428–455), his reflections on poetic craft and philosophical old age to Julius Florus (II. ii, scan leaves 457–477), and his celebrated epistolary treatise on dramatic and poetic propriety addressed to Calpurnius Piso and his two sons (Ars Poetica, scan leaves 478–525). Translated into rhythmic English prose with analytical introductions to each epistle, explanatory footnotes, and an Index of Proper Names by Henry Rushton Fairclough (1862–1938), Professor of Classical Literature at Stanford University (first printed 1926, revised and reprinted 1929, London: William Heinemann Ltd.; prepared from the unmodified 1942 impression of the 1929 revised text, scan leaves 9–10, 13–14), this edition slice presents the complete English Epistles and Ars Poetica alongside Fairclough's Preface, General Introduction (scan leaves 13–36, pp. vii–xxx), and Index of Proper Names (scan leaves 527–545, pp. 491–509)."
