Verania, the wife of that Piso who was adopted by Galba, lay extremely ill : upon this occasion Regulus made her a visit. By the way, mark the assurance of the man, to visit a sick lady to whom he was so extremely odious, and to whose husband he was a declared enemy ! Even barely to enter her house would have been impudent enough ; but he had the confidence to go much farther, and very familiarly placed himself by her bed's side. He began with inquiring what day and hour she was born ? Being informed of these particulars, he composes his countenance, fixes his eyes, mutters something to himself, counts on his fingers ; nothing comes of it. After keeping the poor lady on tenterhooks, “ You are,” says he, “ in one of your climacterics ; however, you will get over it. But for your greater satisfaction, I will consult with a certain diviner, whose skill I have frequently experienced.” Accordingly away he goes, sacrifices, and returns with the strongest assurances that inspection of the victim's entrails confirmed what he had predicted by astrology. Upon this the good woman, made credulous by her dangerous state, calls for her will, and gives Regulus a handsome legacy. Some time afterwards her distemper increased ; and in her last moments she exclaimed against this perfidious, worse than perjured, wretch, who had wished every curse might befall his son, if what he promised her was not true.
