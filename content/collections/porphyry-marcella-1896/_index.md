  "Written around A.D. 303 during a forced absence on \"the affairs of the Greeks\" roughly ten months after his late-life marriage in Rome in A.D. 302 (Introduction, scan leaves 39–41, pp. 35–37; Letter § 1–4, scan leaves 57–60, pp. 53–56), Porphyry of Tyre's philosophical letter to his wife Marcella—a Roman widow and mother of five daughters and two sons—unfolds a deeply personal Neo-Platonic consolation. Across thirty-five surviving sections (scan leaves 57–83, pp. 53–[79], breaking off abruptly where the Milan manuscript ends), Porphyry counsels Marcella not to grieve for his bodily absence, tracing the ascent of the rational soul toward the divine through the four cardinal virtues of faith, truth, love, and hope, and distinguishing inward purity of mind from outward sacrifice.",
  "Lost for centuries until Cardinal Angelo Mai discovered a fifteenth-century manuscript codex in the Ambrosian Library at Milan in 1816 (frontispiece plate, scan leaf 6; Introduction, scan leaves 41, 53–54, pp. 37, 49–50), the letter received its first English translation in this 1896 London edition published by George Redway (title leaf, scan leaf 7). Classical scholar Alice Zimmern (1855–1939) prepared her rendering from Mai's text alongside August Nauck's Teubner edition, prefaced by an essay on late-antique Neo-Platonism by Richard Garnett, C.B., LL.D., Keeper of Printed Books at the British Museum (Preface, scan leaves 9–20, pp. 5–16, dated October 1895), and Zimmern's own historical Introduction on Alexandria, Plotinus, Porphyry, and his treatise (scan leaves 21–54, pp. 17–50)."
