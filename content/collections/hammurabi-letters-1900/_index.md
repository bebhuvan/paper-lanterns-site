  "Excavated from Old Babylonian clay tablets in southern Mesopotamia and preserved in the British Museum, the eighty-three cuneiform letters translated in this volume offer an immediate, unvarnished record of royal administration under the First Dynasty of Babylon in the eighteenth and seventeenth centuries B.C. (dated on the 1900 title page to 'about B.C. 2200' from Nabonidus's round figures before twentieth-century Mari and Venus-tablet chronology placed Hammurabi's reign at c. 1792–1750 B.C.; Introduction, scan leaves 80–81, pp. lxx–lxxi). Fifty-five royal dispatches pass directly from Hammurabi (Ḫammurabi) to Sin-idinnam, his high official in southern Babylonia at Larsam (Section I, scan leaves 85–198, pp. 3–116), directing troop movements (Letter I, scan leaf 85, p. 3), the conveyance and return of Elamite goddesses (Letters II–III, scan leaves 88–93, pp. 6–11), the insertion of an intercalary second Elul in the calendar (Letter IV, scan leaf 94, p. 12), the dredging of the Damanum and Erech canals (Letters V–VII, scan leaves 96–101, pp. 14–19), bribery inquiries and restitution against money-lenders (Letters VIII–XIII, scan leaves 102–114, pp. 20–32), temple and tax audits (Letters XIX–XXI, scan leaves 127–133, pp. 45–51), timber and grain convoys to Babylon (Letters XXII–XXVIII, scan leaves 134–148, pp. 52–66), royal sheep-shearing accounts (Letters XXX–XXXV, scan leaves 150–162, pp. 68–80), and arrest warrants for insubordinate officials (Letters LIII–LV, scan leaves 192–198, pp. 110–116). Sections II–VI add twenty-six royal letters of Hammurabi's successors—Samsu-iluna (6 letters, scan leaves 199–211, pp. 117–129), Abēšu’ / Abi-eshuh (13 letters, scan leaves 212–238, pp. 130–156), Ammiditana / Ammi-ditana (2 letters, scan leaves 239–243, pp. 157–161), and Ammizaduga / Ammi-saduqa (5 sheep-shearing summonses to Ibni-Sin, scan leaves 244–249, pp. 162–167)—addressed largely to the merchant guild (Kār-Sippar) and judges of Sippar, together with two Miscellaneous Letters involving Sin-idinnam, the court in Kutalla, and Sin-idinnam's wife Aḫatim (scan leaves 250–253, pp. 168–171).",
  "Assyriologist Leonard William King, M.A., F.S.A. (1869–1919), Assistant in the Department of Egyptian and Assyrian Antiquities at the British Museum, edited the cuneiform autographs in Volumes I–II (1898–1900) and completed the work with Volume III: English Translations, etc., issued as Vol. VIII of Luzac’s Semitic Text and Translation Series (London: Luzac and Co., printed by Stephen Austin and Sons at Hertford, Preface dated September 1st, 1900; scan leaves 9–20). Alongside paired Akkadian transliterations (Obv./Rev.), line-numbered English translations, and historical headnotes for all eighty-three letters, Volume III contains the Frontispiece portrait sculpture of Hammurabi (Brit. Mus., No. 22,454; scan leaf 14), King's Introduction on Old Babylonian postal convoys, clay tablets and envelopes, greeting formulae, provincial administration, and First Dynasty chronology (scan leaves 27–81, pp. xix–lxxi), Royal Inscriptions of Hammurabi and later kings of the dynasty (Sections VII–IX, scan leaves 254–293, pp. 172–211), the Chronicle of the Kings of the First Dynasty (Section X, scan leaves 294–335, pp. 212–253), the Appendix (scan leaves 336–340, pp. 254–258), and complete Babylonian and Sumerian Vocabularies and Indices (scan leaves 341–417, pp. 259–335). This Paper Lanterns edition preserves King's Assyriological diacritics (Ḫ, Š, Ṣ, Ṭ, Ḳ, macrons), determinative superscripts, outer-margin shoulder notes, and critical footnotes verbatim.",
