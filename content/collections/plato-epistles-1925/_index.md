  "Arranged in chronological order rather than traditional manuscript sequence (Table of Contents, scan leaf 9; Introduction, scan leaf 11, p. 1), the thirteen epistles transmitted under the name of Plato (427–347 B.C.; Chronological Table, scan leaf 23, p. 13) center on the dramatic entanglement of Athenian philosophy with Syracusan politics between 367 and 350 B.C. Beginning with the chatty court letter to Dionysius II after Plato's second Sicilian journey (Epistle XIII, 366 B.C., scan leaf 28, p. 18) and continuing through Epistles II, XI, X, IV, and III to the monumental autobiographical apology and philosophical digression addressed to the friends of the assassinated Dion (Epistle VII, 353 B.C., scan leaves 71–123, pp. 61–113), the constitutional counsel of Epistle VIII (352 B.C., scan leaf 126, p. 116), and the late letter of friendship to Hermias, Erastus, and Coriscus (Epistle VI, 350 B.C., scan leaf 137, p. 127), the volume concludes with the four letters classed by the translator as spurious—Epistles I, V, IX, and XII (scan leaves 140–147, pp. 130–137).",
