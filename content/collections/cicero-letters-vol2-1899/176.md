I, however, had been told that he had gone either to join Pompey or into Spain. What the truth of the matter is I should like very much to know: for it affects the question on which I am now deliberating, that, if Domitius, at any rate, has failed to find an exit from Italy, Gnæus should understand that my own departure from Italy is not easy, occupied as it now is throughout with arms and garrisons, especially in the winter season. For if it had been a more convenient season of the year, I might have sailed even on the Lower Sea. As it is, a passage is impossible except by the Upper Sea, to which my road is closed. Be good enough to inquire, therefore, about both Domitius and Lentulus. No rumour has come as yet from Brundisium, and to-day is the 9th, on which (or on the day before) I imagine that Cæsar has reached Brundisium. For he halted at Arpi on the 1st. But if you choose to believe Postumus, he was intending to pursue Gnæus. For by a calculation of the state of the weather and days he concluded that the latter had already crossed. I said I didn't think Cæsar would have crews: Postumus felt confident on that point, and all the more, because Cæsar's liberality had been heard of by shipowners. But it cannot now be long before I learn the entire state of affairs at Brundisium."
