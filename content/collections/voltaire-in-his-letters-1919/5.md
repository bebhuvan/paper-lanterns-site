      "t": "[In 1723, Voltaire, when a guest in the country house of his friend, M. de Maisons, at St. Germain's, developed smallpox. This letter has a curious interest in its revelation of the medical science, and ignorance, of the day—especially in reference to the treatment and the (supposed) course of the then most inevitable of all diseases—and in showing Voltaire's (comparative) enlightenment on the subject. For this was the epoch when men died like flies, not from disease, but from the doctor, and when Dr. Tronchin, one of the few wise medical men of the age, had crystallised his professional advice into the phrase, “Dare to do nothing; fear the physicians more than the disease.” Even in smallpox, Voltaire dared to do—almost—nothing, and so saved a frail body and one of the most vigorous minds in history for another five and forty years of Herculean labours. It will be noticed that, apparently, no disinfection of the sick room was attempted, so that the fire which accidentally destroyed it was a blessing in disguise."
