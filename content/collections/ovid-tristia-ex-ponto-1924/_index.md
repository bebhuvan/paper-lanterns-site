  "Banished from Rome in late A.D. 8 at the age of fifty by personal edict of the Emperor Augustus—relegated for 'two offences, a poem and a mistake' (the Ars Amatoria and an undisclosed indiscretion; Introduction, scan leaves 22–28, pp. xviii–xxiv; scan leaf 38, p. xxxiv)—Publius Ovidius Naso (43 B.C.–A.D. 17/18) spent his remaining years at Tomis (modern Constanța), a frontier Greek port on the western shore of the Black Sea among Getic and Sarmatian tribes (scan leaves 30–34, pp. xxvi–xxx). From that bleak exile he sent back two collections of elegiac verse-epistles totaling ninety-six poems: the five books of Tristia ('Sorrows', 50 elegies, A.D. 8–12; scan leaves 49–309 odd, pp. 1–261), withholding the names of living Roman friends lest correspondence endanger them while addressing Book II directly to Augustus as a 578-line literary defence (scan leaves 105–147 odd, pp. 57–99); and the four books of Epistulae ex Ponto ('Letters from the Black Sea', 46 epistles, A.D. 12–16; scan leaves 311–537 odd, pp. 263–489), openly naming his Roman correspondents—Brutus, Paullus Fabius Maximus, Marcus Aurelius Cotta Maximus, Marcus Valerius Messalinus, Publius Pomponius Graecinus, Curtius Atticus, Sextus Pompeius, Germanicus Caesar, Albinovanus Pedo, his wife, and fellow poets.",
  "Prepared from the first impression published in 1924 (London: William Heinemann; New York: G. P. Putnam's Sons, MCMXXIV; title leaf, scan leaf 7; colophon, scan leaf 559, p. 511), this English prose translation and critical apparatus are the work of Arthur Leslie Wheeler (1871–1932), Alumnae Professor of Latin at Bryn Mawr College. The edition includes the Table of Contents (scan leaf 9, p. v), Wheeler's historical and manuscript Introduction with Selected Bibliography (scan leaves 11–48, pp. vii–xliv), all ninety-six poems of Tristia Books I–V and Ex Ponto Books I–IV with explanatory footnotes (scan leaves 49–537 odd, pp. 1–489), and the analytical Index of proper names and subjects (scan leaves 539–559, pp. 491–511)."
