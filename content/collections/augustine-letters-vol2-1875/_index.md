  "Volume II of the Letters of Saint Augustine (Vol. XIII of T. & T. Clark's 'Works of Aurelius Augustine,' edited by the Rev. Marcus Dods and published in Edinburgh in November 1875; scan leaves 7, 9–11) completes the Rev. J. G. Cunningham's English translation of the correspondence of Augustine, Bishop of Hippo. Spanning Benedictine Letters XCV through CCLXIX (A.D. 408–429), this volume captures Augustine's mature theological, pastoral, and civic engagement across late Roman North Africa and the wider Mediterranean world.",
  "As explained in the Prefatory Note (scan leaves 13–14, p. vi), Cunningham and Dods selected 160 of the 272 letters in the Benedictine edition across Volumes I and II, excluding letters already covered in the series' Donatist, anti-Pelagian, and Trinitarian volumes. Volume II presents 76 numbered items in the Table of Contents (scan leaves 15–16, pp. vii–viii)—75 translated letters plus Letter CCXIII, Augustine's official ecclesiastical record (gesta) designating Eraclius as his successor—including Augustine's celebrated exchanges with St. Jerome on Scripture and the origin of the soul, Flavius Marcellinus on Christianity and the Roman state after the sack of Rome, Count Boniface, Anicia Faltonia Proba on prayer, Evodius of Uzalis, and Possidius, followed by five scholarly indices (scan leaves 483–496, pp. 467–480)."
